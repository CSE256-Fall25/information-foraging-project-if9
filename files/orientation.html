Orientation

How we support the transition to school

Our program is child-centered, play-based, and process-oriented. Teachers ease children into new routines with warm relationships, small-group play, and hands-on activities that let each child engage at their own pace. We value open communication with families and set developmentally appropriate expectations as children settle in.

What to expect during Orientation week

  • Orientation / Visiting Day: A one-hour classroom visit for children and parents to meet teachers and classmates and hear a brief overview of the year (typically the day after Labor Day).
  • Small-group first days: The class is divided in half; each half attends one shortened day so children can start in a smaller group before everyone attends together.
  • All students attend: Regular schedule begins that Friday following the small-group days.

Key early-year dates (example schedule)

  • Popsicles in the Park – Back to School Social: late August (e.g., Aug 28, 5:30 p.m.).
  • Orientation for parents and children: early September (e.g., Sept 3).
  • Small-group student days: mid-week (e.g., Sept 4–5).
  • Regular hours for all children begin: that Friday (e.g., Sept 6).
  • Bear Tracks begins: first days the following week (e.g., PM Sept 9, AM Sept 10).

For the current year’s dates, see the Calendar or the printable calendar.

What to bring / day-one readiness

  • Clothing: Comfortable play clothes plus at least one extra labeled set (outdoor play is daily; dress for weather).
  • Snacks: School provides a healthy snack (fruit, crackers, water).
  • Health & Safety: Follow illness policy (home until symptom-free and off medication for ~24 hours; physician note may be required for certain conditions).
  • Allergies: We operate a peanut- and tree-nut–free program; inform teachers and the office of any known allergies.

Our approach in the first weeks

Teachers build routines through centers (art, science, dramatic play, blocks, manipulatives, literacy), ask open-ended questions, and scaffold social–emotional skills like collaboration and problem-solving, helping children feel safe and confident as school begins.

Stay connected

Expect ongoing communication via newsletters, the website, email, and phone calls; parent–teacher conferences are held during the year, with additional meetings as needed.
